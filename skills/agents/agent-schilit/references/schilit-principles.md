## The 7 Classic Financial Shenanigans

Howard M. Schilit, Ph.D., CPA, founder of the Center for Financial Research & Analysis (CFRA), identified seven primary categories of financial statement manipulation:

### 1. Recording Revenue Too Soon
- Recording revenue before terms are finalized or services completed.
- Shipping goods before customer confirmation or bill-and-hold transactions.
- Ballooning Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) relative to revenue growth.

### 2. Recording Bogus Revenue
- Recording sales that lack economic substance or transactions with related parties.
- Recording supplier rebates as revenue before earned.
- Circular transactions or barter deals.

### 3. Boosting Income with One-Time Gains
- Selling undervalued assets or investments and classifying the proceeds within operating income.
- Recording balance sheet reserve reversals as operational income.

### 4. Shifting Current Expenses to a Later Period
- Improperly capitalizing normal operating expenses as intangible or fixed assets.
- Amortizing costs too slowly or failing to write down impaired assets.

### 5. Failing to Record or Improperly Reducing Liabilities
- Leaving liabilities off-balance-sheet via unconsolidated special purpose vehicles.
- Releasing questionable accruals or provisions into income.

### 6. Shifting Current Revenue to a Later Period
- Creating "cookie jar" reserves during boom periods to smooth earnings during bad quarters.

### 7. Shifting Future Expenses to the Current Period
- "Big Bath" restructuring write-offs to clear the decks so future quarters look artificially profitable.

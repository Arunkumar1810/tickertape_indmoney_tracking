# Peter Lynch: Growth-At-A-Reasonable-Price (GARP) & Common Sense Investing

> *“Know what you own, and know why you own it.”* — Peter Lynch, *One Up on Wall Street* (1989)  
> *“Go for a business that any idiot can run — because sooner or later, any idiot is probably going to run it.”* — Peter Lynch

## Core Lynch Tenets

### 1. Growth At A Reasonable Price (GARP) & The PEG Ratio
Peter Lynch established that the P/E ratio of any company that's fairly priced will equal its growth rate:
$$\text{PEG Ratio} = \frac{\text{P/E}}{\text{EPS Growth Rate (\%) } }$$
- **$\text{PEG} \le 0.50$**: Screaming growth bargain / exceptional buy.
- **$\text{PEG} \le 1.00$**: Fair and attractive GARP territory.
- **$\text{PEG} > 2.00$**: Overpriced growth bubble / excessive multiple stretch.

### 2. Yield-Adjusted PEG Ratio & Fair Value Line
For dividend-paying stalwarts, dividends contribute directly to total shareholder return:
$$\text{Yield-Adjusted PEG} = \frac{\text{P/E}}{\text{EPS Growth Rate} + \text{Dividend Yield}}$$
$$\text{Lynch Fair Value Line} = (\text{EPS Growth Rate} + \text{Dividend Yield}) \times \text{EPS}$$

### 3. The 6 Company Lifecycle Archetypes
1. **Fast Growers**: Aggressive companies expanding EPS at 20%+ per year. Can be multi-baggers if bought at reasonable PEG.
2. **Stalwarts**: Established multi-billion-dollar giants growing 10% to 19% annually. Offer good protection during recessions.
3. **Slow Growers**: Large, mature companies growing 2% to 9% that pay generous dividends (e.g. electric utilities).
4. **Cyclicals**: Companies whose sales and profits rise and fall in regular or irregular cycles (autos, airlines, steel, chemicals). Timing is paramount.
5. **Turnarounds**: Battered or depressed companies on the brink of bankruptcy or restructuring. Performance is independent of market direction.
6. **Asset Plays**: Companies sitting on hidden assets (cash, real estate, patents, timberland) exceeding their enterprise valuation.

### 4. Operational Inventory Velocity
When inventories grow faster than sales ($\Delta_{\text{inventory}} > \Delta_{\text{sales}}$), it is an ominous red flag indicating channel stuffing, unsold inventory accumulation, or weakening customer demand.

### 5. Common Sense & Circle of Competence
- Never invest in an idea you can't illustrate with a crayon.
- Avoid hot stocks in hot industries, whisper stocks, and companies pursuing "diworseification".
- Maintain disciplined cash till reserves and never over-leverage a portfolio.
